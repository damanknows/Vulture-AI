\begin{abstract}
Automated vulnerability prioritization is challenging because static severity scores do not capture exploitation activity or deployment context, while language-model assistants risk hallucinating or corrupting risk values. Vulture-AI separates deterministic scoring---the Vulture Risk Score (VRS), combining CVSS, EPSS, CISA KEV, network exposure, and asset criticality---from retrieval-augmented language-model explanation. To avoid the specific label circularity of our author-assigned SSVC-inspired benchmark and reduce temporal leakage, we construct the Temporal Exploitation Outcome Benchmark (TEOB): 7,927 non-rejected NVD CVEs published in June 2026 with NVD features reconstructed strictly prior to \texttt{2026-07-01T00:00:00Z} and daily EPSS queried for \texttt{date=2026-07-01}, evaluated against 90-day future CISA KEV additions ($n=11$, 0.139\% prevalence). Because FIRST EPSS is archived at calendar-day granularity (and \texttt{2026-06-30} omits late-June-30 CVEs), exact intraday availability of EPSS at \texttt{00:00:00Z} cannot be verified, leaving a temporal-validity limitation for EPSS. Using unrounded scores and exact tie-aware expected metrics, VRS achieves NDCG@10 of 0.2083 and Average Precision (AP) of 0.1104, compared with CVSS-only (0.0317, 0.0259), EPSS-only (0.0948, 0.0449), and a study-defined CVSS+EPSS+KEV baseline (0.1795, 0.0928). Under an unadjusted 1,000-iteration paired stratified bootstrap, VRS shows nominally significant gains over CVSS-only on Recall@50 ($+0.2193$, 95\% CI $[0.0152, 0.4684]$) and over EPSS-only on AP ($+0.0655$, $[0.0089, 0.1982]$), but all confidence intervals against CVSS+EPSS+KEV include zero; moreover, because pre-cutoff KEV is zero and historical asset context defaults to 0.5, TEOB evaluates only a reduced CVSS+EPSS configuration ($0.25C + 0.20E$). A separate 41-CVE exploratory policy-concordance analysis (including corrected v2 KEV flags and context baselines) is reported in Results with its circularity limitations. In a 5-payload stub-LLM test, the authoritative score was preserved (5/5 with a KEV priority floor), while a prototype keyword guardrail detected 9 of 25 retrieved-content attacks (FPR 0.30). Semantic explanation quality and live-network scanner-to-CVE recall remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Security teams face more vulnerability findings than they can remediate, and severity scores alone do not separate urgent threats from benign exposure. CVSS base scores estimate intrinsic technical severity under worst-case assumptions~\cite{first2019cvss}. The CVSS v4.0 specification states that a base score is not a complete measure of risk and that threat and environmental metrics must be supplied by the consumer~\cite{first2023cvss4}. Distinct prioritization dimensions---intrinsic severity, exploitation likelihood, observed exploitation, asset-specific operational context, and policy-based decision rules---play complementary roles in triage.

Two public intelligence feeds address threat activity: EPSS estimates the 30-day probability of exploitation activity~\cite{first2024epss, jacobs2021epss}, while CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog tracks vulnerabilities with reliable evidence of active exploitation~\cite{cisa2024kev}. Neither feed encodes local asset exposure or criticality, and CISA recommends using KEV within a structured framework such as Stakeholder-Specific Vulnerability Categorization (SSVC)~\cite{spring2020ssvc, cisa2024ssvc}.

Language-model assistants can summarize evidence for analysts, but they introduce two risks: (i) a model may hallucinate or alter numeric risk scores, and (ii) retrieved text is untrusted and vulnerable to indirect prompt injection~\cite{greshake2023indirect}. Vulture-AI addresses this boundary through architectural separation: a deterministic risk engine computes the authoritative VRS from structured inputs, while an LLM generates a structured JSON explanation that undergoes limited regex-based prompt-injection screening and evidence checks, with the authoritative numerical score and KEV priority floor enforced independently by deterministic code. We do not claim VRS as a new scoring theory; rather, our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Auditable system integration and score-integrity boundary.} VRS combines CVSS, EPSS, KEV, exposure, and criticality in a deterministic weighted sum with a KEV urgent bucket, while recomputing the authoritative score and enforcing a KEV priority floor after the LLM call (Sections~\ref{sec:arch}--\ref{sec:method}).
    \item \textbf{Outcome-based temporal evaluation (TEOB).} We construct a 7,927-CVE June 2026 cohort with strict pre-cutoff NVD feature reconstruction (\texttt{2026-07-01T00:00:00Z}) and tie-aware evaluation against 90-day future CISA KEV additions, avoiding the specific label circularity of the author-labelled benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
    \item \textbf{Transparent empirical scope.} Alongside TEOB, we report exploratory 41-CVE policy-concordance results, prototype guardrail rates, score-integrity checks, and unmeasured capabilities (explanation quality and CPE matching accuracy).
\end{enumerate}

We investigate three research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Temporal outcome ranking):} How does VRS rank June 2026 CVEs for 90-day future CISA KEV inclusion relative to CVSS-only, EPSS-only, KEV-first, and CVSS+EPSS+KEV under pre-cutoff NVD feature reconstruction and day-level EPSS?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Exploratory policy concordance):} How closely does VRS align with an author-labelled SSVC-inspired rubric, and how much of that alignment is driven by environmental context versus simpler context baselines?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Guardrail and score integrity):} Does the prototype keyword guardrail detect tested injection patterns, and is the authoritative VRS preserved under adversarial LLM outputs? Semantic explanation quality is out of scope of the empirical evaluation because no live judge endpoint with quota or human SOC-analyst ratings is available.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Severity, Likelihood, and Decision Frameworks}
CVSS v3.1 and v4.0 measure technical severity and optionally incorporate Threat and Environmental modifiers~\cite{first2019cvss, first2023cvss4}, whereas EPSS forecasts 30-day exploitation probability~\cite{first2024epss, jacobs2021epss} and CISA KEV records confirmed exploitation~\cite{cisa2024kev}. SSVC maps exploitation, automatability, technical impact, and mission prevalence to four discrete actions~\cite{spring2020ssvc, cisa2024ssvc}. VRS differs from SSVC by producing a continuous score inside a binary KEV bucket; we evaluate a vendored SSVC tree on the 41-CVE exploratory benchmark in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Composite and LLM-assisted triage}
Recent composite and context-aware prioritization frameworks integrate multiple threat and asset signals. Vulnerability Management Chaining~\cite{shimizu2026chaining} chains CISA KEV, EPSS thresholds, and CVSS severity into a staged decision tree to filter scanner backlogs, whereas VRS uses a continuous weighted sum with a KEV urgent bucket. For enterprise context, Scott and Cohen~\cite{scott2025context} combine internal endpoint attributes (privilege, protective controls, exposure) with EPSS and KEV, whereas VRS abstracts context into normalized $(N, A) \in [0,1]^2$ inputs. In LLM-assisted triage, Al~Haddad et al.~\cite{alhaddad2025llmssvc} prompt LLMs to predict SSVC decision points directly and report a tendency toward risk over-prediction; by contrast, Vulture-AI never delegates scoring or priority assignment to the LLM, restricting it to post-validated explanation. We compare conceptually; we did not re-run these systems as baselines, and we state this explicitly.

\subsection{RAG Security and Prompt Injection}
Retrieval-augmented generation grounds LLM responses in external records~\cite{lewis2020rag}, but indirect prompt injection can hijack model behaviour via retrieved text~\cite{greshake2023indirect, liu2024formalizing}. Defenses such as Spotlighting~\cite{hines2024spotlighting}, StruQ~\cite{chen2024struq}, and SecAlign~\cite{chen2024secalign} mark provenance or train models to separate data from instructions. Vulture-AI does not implement those model-level defenses; instead, it pairs a deterministic post-validation regex filter over retrieved content with an architectural boundary that recomputes numeric scores outside the LLM.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
Vulture-AI consists of four implemented stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scanning and CPE matching (\texttt{scanner.py}, \texttt{cve\_matcher.py}).} An allowlisted Nmap version scan (\texttt{nmap -sV}) extracts service/product/version strings. \texttt{normalize\_service\_string} strips distribution suffixes and patch tails to build CPE~2.3 candidate URIs (\texttt{build\_cpe\_uris}) for NVD lookup, falling back to NVD keyword search (capped at 10 candidates) when CPE lookup yields no matches. False positives (over-broad keyword matches) and false negatives (non-standard banners) are unmeasured.
    \item \textbf{Feature extraction.} Each matched CVE is enriched with NVD CVSS v3.x, FIRST EPSS probability, and CISA KEV membership; network exposure $N$ and asset criticality $A$ are supplied per finding.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic scoring (\texttt{risk\_engine.py}).} \texttt{compute\_vrs} computes the authoritative VRS (Equation~\ref{eq:vrs}) and assigns each finding to an \texttt{urgent} (KEV-listed), \texttt{standard}, or \texttt{unavailable} (missing CVSS) bucket.
    \item \textbf{Guardrailed explanation (\texttt{retrieval.py}, \texttt{vulture\_chatbot.py}, \texttt{evidence\_validator.py}).} Retrieval performs exact CVE lookup or case-insensitive keyword substring matching over the local dataset (no vector store or embeddings). The LLM outputs JSON parsed by Pydantic (\texttt{ChatbotResponse}, which silently ignores extra fields and accepts any priority string). \texttt{evidence\_validator.validate} checks retrieved document content against five case-insensitive regex patterns and verifies that CVE IDs and floating-point numbers in the explanation appear in retrieved evidence (within 0.01 tolerance). Finally, \texttt{chat\_with\_validation} overwrites \texttt{vrs\_score} with \texttt{compute\_vrs} and enforces a deterministic KEV priority floor (\texttt{priority = "high"} for \texttt{urgent} findings).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Vulture Risk Score}
VRS is a deterministic linear combination normalized to $[0, 100]$:
\begin{equation}
\text{VRS} = 100 \times \left( w_C C + w_E E + w_K K + w_N N + w_A A \right)
\label{eq:vrs}
\end{equation}
where $C = \text{CVSS}/10 \in [0,1]$, $E \in [0,1]$ is EPSS probability, $K \in \{0,1\}$ is KEV status, $N \in [0,1]$ is network exposure, and $A \in [0,1]$ is asset criticality. Frozen exploratory weights (\texttt{vrs-v1}) are $w_C=0.25$, $w_E=0.20$, $w_K=0.20$, $w_N=0.20$, and $w_A=0.15$. Missing CVSS yields \texttt{None} (\texttt{unavailable}); missing EPSS defaults to $E=0.0$; missing $N$ or $A$ defaults to $0.5$.

\subsection{Study-Defined Combined Baseline}
To evaluate an unweighted three-signal composite constructed specifically for this study (rather than an existing published method), \texttt{compute\_cvss\_epss\_kev} in \texttt{risk\_engine.py} defines:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{comb}} = 100 \times \left(\tfrac{1}{3} C + \tfrac{1}{3} E + \tfrac{1}{3} K\right)
\label{eq:comb}
\end{equation}
where $C = \text{CVSS}/10$, $E \in [0,1]$ (defaulting to $0.0$ when EPSS is missing), and $K \in \{0,1\}$. When CVSS is missing, $S_{\text{comb}}$ returns \texttt{None} (placed in the bottom tied block in TEOB, or sorted last by ascending CVE ID on the 41-CVE set).

\subsection{Primary Evaluation: TEOB Protocol}
\label{sec:teob_method}
To avoid the specific label circularity of the author-assigned SSVC-inspired benchmark and reduce temporal leakage, we construct TEOB (\texttt{experiments/build\_temporal\_benchmark.py}) with cutoff $T_0 = \text{\texttt{2026-07-01T00:00:00Z}}$ and a 90-day observation horizon ($2026\text{-}07\text{-}02$ through $2026\text{-}09\text{-}29$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cohort selection:} All NVD \texttt{cves/2.0} records published in $[2026\text{-}06\text{-}01\text{T}00:00:00\text{Z}, 2026\text{-}07\text{-}01\text{T}00:00:00\text{Z})$ ($8,001$ CVEs), deduplicated by CVE ID, excluding \texttt{Rejected} ($59$) and pre-cutoff KEV entries with \texttt{dateAdded <= 2026-07-01} ($15$), yielding $7,927$ eligible CVEs.
    \item \textbf{Historical CVSS reconstruction:} If \texttt{lastModified} $< T_0$, current NVD CVSS v3.x is unchanged. Otherwise, NVD \texttt{cvehistory/2.0} CVSS v3.x events at or after $T_0$ are walked backwards in reverse chronological order: \texttt{Added} events are removed (preserving pre-cutoff \texttt{Secondary} CNA scores when \texttt{Primary} NVD scores were added post-cutoff), and \texttt{Removed} events restore \texttt{oldValue} vectors via the CVSS v3.x specification formula. Records whose only CVSS v3.x was added at or after $T_0$ are set to missing (\texttt{added\_after\_cutoff\_excluded}); CVSS v4.0-only records are marked \texttt{missing\_in\_nvd}.
    \item \textbf{EPSS and KEV outcomes:} EPSS is queried retrospectively from the FIRST historical archive for calendar date \texttt{date=2026-07-01} without backfilling. Querying \texttt{date=2026-06-30} is incomplete for late-June-30 publications (omitting 8 of 10 sampled \texttt{2026-06-30} CVEs and both \texttt{2026-06-30} positives). Because FIRST EPSS exposes calendar-day granularity only (daily runs are typically published midday UTC with no intraday timestamp), exact intraday availability at \texttt{2026-07-01T00:00:00Z} cannot be verified; consequently, TEOB is not a fully verified point-in-time evaluation for the EPSS feature, and this temporal limitation remains a threat to validity. Positive outcomes ($y=1$) are CISA KEV additions in catalog \texttt{2026.10.13} (released \texttt{2026-10-13}, covering the full horizon) with $2026\text{-}07\text{-}01 < \text{dateAdded} \le 2026\text{-}09\text{-}29$. Non-positive records ($y=0$) are labelled \emph{``not observed in KEV during the horizon''}, never \emph{``not exploited''}.
    \item \textbf{Effective VRS reduction in TEOB:} Because $K=0$ for all eligible CVEs and historical asset context is unavailable ($N=A=0.5$), Equation~\ref{eq:vrs} reduces to $\text{VRS} = 25C + 20E + 17.5$. TEOB therefore evaluates only the CVSS+EPSS weighting, not $K$, $N$, or $A$.
    \item \textbf{Missing-CVSS handling across methods:} All five methods rank the identical $7{,}927$-CVE population ($P=11$). For the $1{,}244$ records lacking historical CVSS v3.x (including $2$ of the $11$ positives), \texttt{CVSS-only}, \texttt{CVSS+EPSS+KEV}, and \texttt{VRS} (\texttt{unavailable} bucket) assign \texttt{None} ($-\infty$), forming a $1{,}244$-record bottom tied block; \texttt{KEV-first} assigns $0.0$, merging them with the $4$ records having $\text{CVSS}=0.0$ into a $1{,}248$-record bottom block; and \texttt{EPSS-only} scores all $7{,}927$ records using their \texttt{2026-07-01} EPSS probabilities.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tie-Aware Metrics and Paired Bootstrap}
Because 63 eligible CVEs tie at $\text{CVSS}=10.0$ in TEOB, deterministic CVE-ID tie-breaking would make top-10 metrics an artifact of string ordering. In \texttt{evaluate\_temporal.py}, all methods are ranked on unrounded float64 scores, and we compute exact analytical expected NDCG@10, Precision@10 (denominator $\min(10, N)$), Recall@\{10, 50, 100\}, and Average Precision over uniform random permutations within tied score blocks. Uncertainty is estimated via 1,000-iteration paired stratified bootstrap (seed \texttt{20261127}), resampling positives and negatives separately with identical indices across methods; confidence intervals are not adjusted for multiple comparisons across metrics and baselines, so intervals excluding zero indicate exploratory \emph{nominal} 95\% significance only.

\subsection{Secondary Exploratory Benchmark (41 CVEs)}
We retain the original 41-record author-labelled benchmark (39 real CVEs, 2 synthetic edge cases; SSVC-inspired grades Act=3, Attend=2, Track=1, Defer=0) solely as an exploratory policy-concordance check~\cite{nvd2023}. Because its labels share inputs with VRS and use a 2026 snapshot, it is reported separately from TEOB. Anonymized code, frozen datasets, and reproduction scripts are available at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/Vulture-AI}.

\subsection{Exploratory 41-CVE Policy Concordance (RQ2)}
On the separate 41-CVE exploratory benchmark (v1, SHA prefix \texttt{27979cc0}), VRS obtains NDCG@10 of 1.0000 and Spearman $\rho$ of 0.9488 (versus CVSS-only 0.7563 / 0.7357, EPSS-only 0.9217 / 0.8237, KEV-first 0.7945 / 0.8247, CVSS+EPSS+KEV 0.9513 / 0.8800, and a vendored, cited adaptation of the CISA/SEI SSVC v2.0 decision tree~\cite{spring2020ssvc, cisa2024ssvc} at 0.6939 / 0.6129; Table~\ref{tab:exploratory} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ranking_and_recall}). The SSVC baseline applies uniform mappings across all 41 records (\texttt{Exploitation} = \texttt{active} if KEV=1, \texttt{poc} if EPSS $\ge 0.1$, else \texttt{none}; \texttt{Technical Impact} = \texttt{total} if CVSS $\ge 9.0$; \texttt{Mission Prevalence} from $A$; uniform defaults \texttt{Automatable=yes}, \texttt{Public Well-being=minimal}; tiers mapped as \texttt{Act}=3, \texttt{Attend}=2, \texttt{Track*}=\texttt{Track}=1 with ascending CVE ID tie-breaking). Under 2{,}000 paired bootstrap resamples (seed \texttt{20261126}), VRS outperforms this SSVC tree by $+0.3061$ ($[0.0938, 0.4754]$) on NDCG@10 and $+0.3359$ ($[0.1300, 0.6242]$) on Spearman $\rho$ (Actionable P@10/R@10 are 1.0000 / 0.3704 for both); across all $2\times2$ combinations of the two default assumptions (\texttt{Automatable} $\times$ \texttt{Public Well-being}), SSVC's Spearman $\rho$ ranges over $[0.6129, 0.7620]$ (\texttt{results\_ssvc.json}). Removing environmental context (\texttt{No Context}) drops $\rho$ to 0.8782, whereas simple context-aware baselines are competitive: \texttt{EPSS$\times$Context} achieves NDCG@10 of 1.0000 and $\rho$ of 0.9334 (paired 95\% CI vs VRS: $[-0.038, 0.080]$, including zero). \section{Discussion}
TEOB demonstrates that combining historical CVSS and EPSS signals improves ranking concordance with 90-day future CISA KEV additions compared with CVSS-only and EPSS-only on select metrics, while remaining statistically indistinguishable from the study-defined \texttt{CVSS+EPSS+KEV} baseline. Crucially, because historical asset context is unavailable and pre-cutoff KEV entries are excluded, TEOB evaluates only $0.25C + 0.20E$ and does \textbf{not} validate VRS's KEV, exposure, or criticality weights. Conversely, the exploratory 41-CVE benchmark (v1 $\rho=0.9488$; corrected v2 $\rho=0.9317$) isolates context sensitivity ($-0.0706$ Spearman drop without context) but suffers from label circularity and ties with \texttt{EPSS$\times$Context} ($\rho=0.9334$); neither v1 nor v2 establishes validated superiority. Together, these findings support Vulture-AI's architectural separation of deterministic scoring from LLM prose, while showing that its prototype regex guardrail (TPR 0.36, FPR 0.30) is insufficient as a standalone injection defense.

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{KEV outcome proxy and EPSS temporal granularity:} Future CISA KEV addition reflects federal-reported exploitation, not exhaustive real-world exploitation or organizational remediation urgency, and a single \texttt{2026.10.13} snapshot cannot detect hypothetical additions later removed before \texttt{2026-10-13}. Moreover, EPSS is archived at calendar-day granularity (\texttt{date=2026-07-01}) with no intraday timestamp; exact availability at \texttt{2026-07-01T00:00:00Z} cannot be verified, so TEOB is not a fully verified point-in-time evaluation for the EPSS feature.
    \item \textbf{Sparse positives, unadjusted CIs, and unequal feature availability:} Only 11 of 7,927 CVEs (0.139\%) entered KEV within 90 days, producing wide, uncertain bootstrap intervals. Confidence intervals are not adjusted for multiple comparisons across metrics and baselines, so nominally significant differences are exploratory. Additionally, 1,244 records (including 518 CVSS v4.0-only CVEs and 2 positives) lack historical CVSS v3.x; because CVSS-dependent methods place them in a bottom tied block whereas \texttt{EPSS-only} ranks them, methods face unequal feature availability.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory benchmark limits:} The 41-CVE set is small, author-graded without independent rater scores, and shares inputs with VRS; weights remain exploratory and uncalibrated. Blind-sampling and agreement-calculation annotation infrastructure is provided; independent rater data were not collected (\texttt{docs/annotation/rater\_protocol.md}, \texttt{PENDING}).
    \item \textbf{Unmeasured components:} Explanation quality (\texttt{explanation\_summary.json}), live-network scanner-to-CVE end-to-end recall (only 36 synthesized CPE-normalization banners were tested), live frontier-LLM guardrail behavior (no active API quota / local model server), and true CVSS v4.0 environmental scoring (\texttt{cves.csv} lacks v4.0 vectors, so Table~\ref{tab:exploratory} uses a v3.1 scalar proxy) remain unmeasured and are left for future work. Neither SSVC nor CVSS v4.0 environmental scoring is run on TEOB because historical context and complete pre-cutoff v4.0 vectors are unavailable at \texttt{2026-07-01} (\texttt{ssvc\_teob\_infeasible.md}, \texttt{cvss\_v4\_teob\_infeasible.md}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
Vulture-AI separates deterministic vulnerability scoring from retrieval-augmented LLM explanation. On the temporally separated 7,927-CVE TEOB cohort, VRS achieved higher point estimates than single-signal baselines (nominally significant on Recall@50 vs CVSS-only and AP vs EPSS-only, unadjusted for multiple comparisons) but did not significantly outperform the study-defined CVSS+EPSS+KEV baseline, and effectively tested only a reduced CVSS+EPSS configuration with calendar-day EPSS granularity. Exploratory 41-CVE concordance (v1/v2) remains limited by label circularity and competitiveness with \texttt{EPSS$\times$Context}. A deterministic KEV priority floor preserved score and KEV-priority integrity across 5 stub-LLM payloads, whereas the prototype keyword guardrail showed weak detection (TPR 0.36, FPR 0.30) and semantic explanation quality remains unmeasured. Multi-month temporal cohorts, independent asset context, stronger prompt-injection defenses, and human explanation evaluations are needed before operational deployment claims can be made.
